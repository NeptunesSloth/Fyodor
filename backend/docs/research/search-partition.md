# Revealed search packets divided among destinations

Nissa's Pilgrimage, Viewpoint Synchronization, Boreas Charger, Verdant Mastery, and Druid of the Emerald Grove share a native library search followed by a revealed packet partition. The search still uses the normal prohibition, Mindcensor depth, and Opposition Agent control and exile rules. Agent-diverted finds physically enter exile and receive exact-object native play permissions before a later die roll or destination choice; they never move a second time. A small adapter assigns the found cards to ordered destination quantities, then returns to the ordinary simultaneous entry and single-shuffle path. Found objects retain exact IDs and zones across saved choices.

The mandatory battlefield quantity is bounded by the number found. Nissa finding only one Forest must put it onto the battlefield; Viewpoint finding only two lands must put both onto the battlefield. Nissa's mastery is checked once as resolution reaches the search, counting instant and/or sorcery cards once each, with the resolving spell still on the stack.

Boreas chooses a qualifying opponent as its leave trigger resolves and reads that opponent's land difference for the search. If no opponent has more lands, it still searches and shuffles but finds zero Plains. The search accepts nonbasic Plains. It uses the trigger controller, including when the departed creature's owner differs.

Verdant's alternative cost is read from the exact source object's native payment record, including on actual spell copies (CR 707.10). This is a copied cost decision, independent of the cast_from=None fact that the copy wasn't cast; ordinary cast-history predicates remain unchanged. If paid, the first found land assignment goes to the chosen opponent, then up to two remaining found lands enter under the spell controller; the rest go to hand. Owner and entry controller remain separate. All assigned battlefield entries use the ordinary entry replacement pipeline together; failed or replaced entry never causes another card to be reassigned.

Druid searches and reveals before rolling its native d20. It rolls even if the search found zero cards or was prohibited. Native extra-die replacements and kept results determine the row, and replay uses recorded randomness instead of rolling again. The middle row requires choosing one found land when two were found; the highest row puts all found lands onto the battlefield.

Entry testing distinguishes Priest/Moonlight's would-be battlefield creature from Cage's creature card in its source library. With Life and Limb, a basic Forest becomes a creature as it enters, so Priest/Moonlight exile it; Cage does not prohibit that Forest because it isn't a creature card in the library.

Official rulings consulted: Nissa's Pilgrimage (2015), Viewpoint Synchronization (2024), Boreas Charger (2018), Verdant Mastery (2021). In particular, Boreas's no-qualifying-opponent search and the smaller-found-set mandatory destination rules are explicit rulings. Canonical fixtures preserve complete DB metadata. Gameplay checks include saved/replayed partition decisions and HTTP/WebSocket privacy before reveal and after cards enter hidden hands.
