# Previous-own-turn combat history

Giant Turtle, Halls of Mist, Goblin Rock Sled, Tangle Kelp, and Weathered Sentinels refer to a particular player's last turn. In multiplayer, that turn is generally not the immediately preceding global turn. An extra turn can make the same player take consecutive turns; a skipped turn never begins and does not replace their previous completed turn (CR 500.7 and 614.10).

The rules state keeps one compact, public completed-turn summary per player: the turn number, exact rules objects declared as attackers during that turn, and actual players attacked by that player. Every completed turn replaces the prior summary, including an empty turn. TurnLog records its active player explicitly; turn order is never reconstructed from seating. Existing current/previous global logs retain their meanings. No hands, draws, or other private turn facts are copied into these summaries.

Native table-step tracking creates even empty logs and archives them at rollover. The engine also idempotently completes the ending turn before computing which permanents stay tapped during the next untap. This matters for consecutive extra turns, because the keep-tapped list is computed before NextTurn is applied. An extra untap during the running current turn leaves the previous completed-own-turn summary unchanged.

Exact object identity implements CR 400.7: blinking or reanimating a card does not preserve its previous object's attack history. Phasing preserves it (CR 702.26). Creatures entering attacking were not declared as attackers and are excluded (CR 508.4). Native card renumbering already rewrites nested typed references. Current control determines whose previous turn Giant Turtle, Halls of Mist, Goblin Rock Sled, and Tangle Kelp consult. The Aura's controller does not replace its host's controller for Tangle Kelp.

Weathered Sentinels receives a destination-dependent defender exception, rather than haste or loss of defender. Its exception permits attacking a qualifying player; it does not permit attacking that player's planeswalker or battle. An independent unconditional defender exception can permit other destinations. Native attack requirement optimizers use the same destination legality. Actual attacks still produce the card's normal +3/+3 and indestructible trigger.

New games explicitly know that players who have not completed a turn have empty prior history. Legacy saves missing this history remain distinguishable from that known-empty case. New history-dependent restrictions stay conservative while required earlier information is unavailable; a native recovery note appears once when an affected card requires it. Read-only views do not modify state or add notes. Completing a player's turn restores known history for that player. Public metadata uses typed models and ordered lists so JSON validation, replay, and separate-process hash seeds agree.

Canonical tests cover all five cards across three players, empty turns, extra turns, skipped turns, same-turn extra untaps, current controller changes, source departure/ability loss, actual Cloudshift and Reality Ripple, native Yore-Tiller entry attacking, defender/haste/destination boundaries, legacy recovery, and two fresh-process restores before the next own turn.
